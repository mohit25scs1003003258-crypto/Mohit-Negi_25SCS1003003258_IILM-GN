This project focuses on analyzing the challenges associated with monoculture agriculture and proposes an Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning–based approach to promote sustainable farming practices. Continuous cultivation of the same crop can lead to soil degradation, nutrient imbalance, reduced biodiversity, increased pest risks, and declining crop productivity. To address these challenges, the project develops a computational framework that integrates soil data, crop history, weather information, and farmer feedback for comprehensive agricultural risk analysis. Various preprocessing and feature extraction techniques are applied to prepare the data, followed by machine learning models such as Random Forest, Decision Tree, and K-Means clustering to classify risk levels, predict soil degradation, and group similar agricultural regions. NLP techniques are also used to analyze farmer feedback and identify common issues such as low yield, pest attacks, soil weakness, and fertilizer dependency. The results demonstrate that regions with lower organic carbon and prolonged monoculture patterns are more likely to fall into high or very-high-risk categories. Overall, this project demonstrates how combining machine learning, clustering, data analysis, and NLP can provide an efficient and scalable solution for identifying agricultural risks, supporting crop rotation and diversification, and improving long-term soil health and sustainable farming practices.
